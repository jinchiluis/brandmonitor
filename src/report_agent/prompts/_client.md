Rules that hold in every step, learned from a hand-made cycle of this report:

1. A date on a page is not the date of the event. Use the event or publication
   day given in the record, and say which. A `lastmod` date is never a
   publication date and never reaches the customer. A platform's submission day
   is not the day it acted. A parliamentary procedure's date is its latest
   step, not its adoption — name the step, not only the date.
2. `body_status: ok` is not evidence. Teasers, podcast blurbs, cookie furniture
   and sidebar text all extract cleanly. If the stored text does not actually
   support a claim, the treatment is `insufficient_evidence`, not a hedge.
3. Several articles about one development are one finding. Trade media restate
   each other within a day or two; an unmerged restatement is a spurious second
   entry in the client's week.
4. Report the number the source reports, in the source's own frame. Total
   capacity after an expansion is not new capacity. Municipalities covered are
   not lockers installed. An order count from an earlier inspection round is
   not this week's penalties.
5. Absence is not evidence. A company that did not answer a journalist is not
   in breach. A story with no follow-up is not resolved. A source that returned
   nothing returned nothing — say so rather than filling the space.
6. Scheduled is not adopted. An agenda, a tabled amendment or a planned vote is
   a date to watch, never a rule in force.
7. Never claim the client's own operations were affected unless a source says
   so. The honest form is the exposure question the client's team must check.
